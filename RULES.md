# Painted Realms rules

Painted Realms is a cooperative, three-encounter adventure. One to ten heroes recover three lantern shards. Everyone wins together; scripted enemies oppose the party. A solo game can include scripted companions. Companions are ordinary party members, and count toward the ten-hero limit.

## On your turn

1. Click a reachable square to move. Each square is five feet; movement follows horizontal and vertical paths. Walls, water, and occupied squares block movement.
2. Take one action: attack, use your class power, guard, or drink a potion. To attack, select an enemy, then press **Roll d20** to start the animated die. The result shows the confirmed d20, hit/miss or critical hit, and damage; press **Continue** to return to the board. Offensive class powers use the same roll prompt; canceling before rolling spends nothing. Reduced motion skips the tumble. You can move before or after your action, within your remaining movement.
3. End your turn. Other heroes and enemies then take their turns.

Other players', companions' and enemies' rolls appear on your screen automatically, one at a time, with their names and confirmed results. Use **Next roll** or **Continue** to move on sooner, or let the display advance automatically. Watching a roll does not spend an action or roll the dice again. Automatic death saves appear too; reduced motion skips the tumble.

Use **Pause auto-advance** to read results at your own pace, **Resume auto-advance** to continue automatically, or **Back to board** to dismiss the queued presentations. These controls only change the display; recent results remain in the adventure log. Incoming rolls wait until you close a character sheet, turn confirmation, or mobile panel.

Ranged attacks need a clear line of sight. Walls block sight; water does not. Knights, clerics, guardians and rogues attack adjacent squares. Rangers attack within five squares; mages, bards and druids within four. Range uses horizontal-plus-vertical distance, matching movement. These are intentionally simple grid conventions.

Each hero begins with two potions and two class-power uses per encounter. Knight: extra damage. Ranger: a longer ranged shot with advantage. Mage: extra arcane damage to one target. Cleric: restore an injured or downed ally's health within four squares. Guardian (shield wall): guard an adjacent ally; until the guardian's next turn, an enemy blow aimed at that ally lands on the guardian instead, as long as the guardian is still beside them. Rogue (sneak attack): a strike that deals much more damage when another hero is next to the target, and less when the rogue fights alone. Bard (inspire): an ally within four squares makes their next attack roll with advantage. Druid (entangle): an enemy within four squares cannot move on its next turn. Powers consume the turn's action. In the hero roster, Warrior heroes play as knights. Potions heal 2d4 + 6 HP and consume an action.

Guard makes attack rolls against you use disadvantage until your next turn. In streamlined mode it also halves incoming damage. A natural 20 on an attack adds another damage die.

Once every enemy is defeated, open the chest from an adjacent square. The party gains a shard and a potion each. Move next to the glowing gateway to continue. Between scenes the party regains some health, refreshes powers, and receives at least two potions each. Leaving the third scene with its shard wins the adventure.

## The Hollow Keepers

The last chapter of every adventure ends with a Keeper, the guardian of the final shard. Each built-in tale has its own: **The Wolf at the Last Light** (Lanternwood), **The Crown’s Dragon** (The Hollow Reach) and **The Tideglass Warden** (Saltreach Harbour). A custom tale’s final guardian is drawn from its landscape’s boss pool by the adventure seed and named after the tale’s world (for example, *The Ridge Giant of Cogmere*; a world name longer than 24 characters is left off).

- **Stronger than the other guardians.** A Keeper has 16 more health and 2 more Armor Class than an ordinary guardian of the same chapter. Its strikes are +5 to hit and deal 1d8 + 2. Speed: wolves and the Thornhound 6, wardens 5, the Dragon, Old Drake, Ridge Giant and Glass Eye 4.
- **A Keeper never guards.** When no hero is in reach, it moves toward the party.
- **Announce, then strike.** On one turn a Keeper announces its signature attack instead of attacking. The tiles it will hit are marked on the board with a hatched, dashed outline. The adventure log names the attack, and a hero standing on marked ground is warned on their turn. On its next turn the attack resolves on exactly those tiles. It never picks a new target or new tiles. Then it takes one ordinary turn (approach and strike) before announcing again.
- **Getting out of the way.** A hero who leaves the marked tiles before the Keeper’s next turn avoids the attack. Guarding halves the damage of the Dragon’s fire and the Warden’s arcs, and gives disadvantage against the Wolf’s pounce. If a Keeper is defeated before its turn, the marked attack is cancelled.

**Lantern Hunt (the Wolf).** The wolf marks the nearest hero it can reach within eight tiles, the full route to them, and its landing tile beside them. On its next turn it runs that route and stops short of anything now standing in the way. If the marked hero is still on the marked tiles and beside the wolf, it pounces: an attack with advantage that adds an extra 1d8 of damage. Otherwise the pounce lands on empty ground and the wolf is **exposed**: −3 Armor Class until its next turn.

**The shrine's light.** At the lantern shrine (chapter III of the Lantern Road), six lit refuge tiles lie before the shrine, marked by warm light, a dotted edge and a sun painted on each tile. Lantern Hunt never enters them: the route and landing stay outside, and a hero standing in the light cannot be marked. A marked hero who steps into the light ends the hunt at its edge: the wolf halts on its landing tile outside the light and is exposed. Only the hunt stops there. The wolf can still move and bite as usual, and other guardians walk in freely. The wolf starts on the same tile below the light whatever the party's size.

**Lanternfire Breath (the Dragon).** The dragon marks a lane three tiles wide and up to six tiles long, aimed in the straight direction that catches the most heroes. Walls cut the lane short, so a hero behind a wall is in cover. On its next turn, every hero still in the lane takes 2d6 + 3 fire damage, with no attack roll.

**Breaking Tide (the Warden).** The Warden marks a numbered arc of three tiles in front of her, a two-tile step toward the hero she faces, and a second arc from there. Once she is at half health or below, the tide adds a third step and arc. On her next turn the arcs break in order, each dealing 1d8 + 2 to heroes standing on it. If a step is blocked when she reaches it, the tide ends there. When defeated, the Warden lowers her glass blade and yields.

**Custom-tale boss pool.** Each pool boss uses one of the three attacks above, under its own name:

| Landscape | Boss | Attack | Works like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woodland | Thornhound | Thorn Rush | Lantern Hunt |
| Woodland | Grey Wolf | The Hunt | Lantern Hunt |
| Ruins | Ridge Giant | Rockfall | Breaking Tide |
| Ruins | Old Drake | Fire Breath | Lanternfire Breath |
| Coast | Glass Eye | Glaring Gaze | Lanternfire Breath |
| Coast | Tide Warden | Breaking Tide | Breaking Tide |

**Wider enemy groups.** Each chapter of a custom tale draws its four kinds of guardian, by seed, from the landscape’s usual four plus a wider group: thornhounds and dawn sentinels in woodland, oathbound guards and sealwax oozes in ruins, glass eyes on the coast. The boss’s own creature never appears as an ordinary guardian. The built-in tales keep their own guardians.

Games saved before the named Keepers keep the earlier untitled Hollow Keeper, both in play and in replay. Custom tales saved before the boss pool keep the Hollow Keeper (with the Keeper’s strength but no signature attack) and their four usual guardians.

## Streamlined mode

The party acts before enemies each round. Attacks hit unless the d20 is a natural 1. Downed heroes skip their turns and can be healed by a cleric. If the encounter ends while a hero is down, the magical lantern revives them at half health. If the entire party is down, the adventure ends in defeat.

## Classic mode — an SRD-inspired subset

This mode adds individual d20 initiative, attack rolls against Armor Class, natural-1 misses and natural-20 hits, advantage/disadvantage, and death saving throws. It is **not the complete Dungeons & Dragons ruleset**. Character statistics, powers, potion healing, cardinal grid movement, encounter recovery, and the three-scene adventure are custom game rules.

A downed hero rolls a d20 at the start of their turn: 10 or higher adds a success; below 10 adds a failure; a natural 1 adds two failures; a natural 20 returns the hero with 1 HP. Three successes stabilize them; three failures put them out until the encounter ends. Stabilized heroes still need healing. The lantern revives fallen party members after a won encounter in both modes, a deliberately forgiving house rule. If everyone is down, the adventure is lost.

Not implemented: full character creation, races/species, official classes and spell lists, levels, spell slots, bonus actions, reactions, opportunity attacks, cover, saving-throw spells, conditions, equipment systems, encumbrance, resting rules, death permanence, or an arbitrary tabletop rule adjudicator. The UI should use “Classic (SRD subset),” not “Full D&D.”

## What is scripted now

Adventure openings have three narrator personalities. In the three built-in tales each chapter opening leads with a clue the party can see (game/lore.ts); custom tales use the general openings. Seeded map details, initiative, and dice rolls are reproducible. Bots select legal actions, heal, attack, move, and collect objectives using fixed rules. No model API is called. The server validates human actions and owns the shared state and random generator. Future narration or action-parsing models should request actions through these same checks; they must not directly set health, dice results, turns, or inventory.
