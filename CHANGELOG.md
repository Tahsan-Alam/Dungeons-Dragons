# Changelog

Record user-visible changes at version checkpoints. Git history and pull requests track the individual edits between them.

## Unreleased

- Long built-in tales now play five distinct places instead of repeating the first two: chapter I, chapter II, two new places on the road, then the Keeper's place. The Lantern Road adds Bramble Crossing (a stream with a new plank bridge, stepping stones and brambles cut back from one bank) and the Moonwell (a shining well in a ring of standing stones, its lantern hook empty); the Hollow Crown adds the Sunken Archive (flooded channels between shelves, one emptied) and the Star Chamber (a brass orrery missing a star); Salt and Silver adds the Tideglass Steps (terraces down to the harbour, fresh glass on every step) and the Mistwater Cove (a smugglers' boat beached with its lantern hooded). Each opens on its own clue. Three new creatures guard them: the Mirewisp, the Star moth and the Mist eel. The between-chapters map shows the party on the road to the third waymark. Saves started earlier keep their places.
- Create your own Game Master: a fourth card on the Game Master step. Name them (or borrow a name), say who they are, choose how they tell it (Hopeful, Wry or Grave) and their shape, what they wear and their colour, with a live preview and a sample line. They tell any tale in the Lantern Marches with the matching narration voice and appear in the setup, the chapter openings and play. The result passes the same validation as an AI-made Game Master; the built-in names stay reserved, and heroes keep no sealed notes with your own Game Master.
- On phones the selected foe's card is two compact rows in the Game Master's slot instead of a large panel over the board: face, name, health and the attack buttons, then the facts as chips in the order that decides an attack (a Keeper's next move, range, to hit, damage, armour). The fight and the board tools stay in view.
- Sealed notes. When a built-in hero sets out with a built-in Game Master, the Game Master hands that hero's player a sealed letter once chapter I's opening closes: break the wax seal to read the secret that ties the hero to the Game Master (one of 48 from the Story Bible). Only that player sees it; companions and custom heroes or Game Masters have none. Later keeps it sealed, and the note stays in your hero sheet either way.
- In dark colour mode the board is at night. The ground is moonlit and a cool veil darkens it toward the edges; every light already on the board (the last lamp, the lit waymark, the shrine, the keep's torches and crown, the district lamp, the lens, the sea door) keeps a halo and throws a warm or teal pool. A hero or foe standing in a pool takes its light as a rim; props out of the light sit in moonlight. Move range, the Keepers' marked tiles, the refuge edge, the chest, the way on and health bars stay above the night. Light mode is unchanged, and night changes no rule.
- The Hollow Crown's and Salt and Silver's chapters II and III are now their approved places. The ridge road: the trail along the drop behind a patched parapet with two gaps, a retaining wall below, banners in the wind and cart ruts to the turn. The hollow keep: an ashlar courtyard, two pairs of pillars that cut the dragon's fire short, the treasury under the worn royal seal and the crown on its plinth. Glassmakers' row: six shuttered workshops with three different fronts, empty brass racks, the paid invoice and the same mark chalked toward the stairs down, a timber quay and a jetty over the harbour. The harbour vaults: arch piers and fallen stumps with side exits between them, the barred rear door with the chest before it, the stairs down from the row and the sea door out. The dragon now draws its head back with its throat lit when it marks the lane, and shows its open jaws after breathing; the Warden winds up with her blade across her body and kneels when she yields. Saves from earlier versions keep their generated maps.
- On phones the board's tools sit in one compact row in the top-right corner instead of a two-row bar across the board.
- The Lantern Road's chapters II and III are now their approved places. The forest road: raised cobbles with a stone lip, a split-rail fence with one gap, the lit and the dark waymark, low water on both sides that blocks movement but not sight, and the road running out between two trunks; the wolf's eyes watch from beyond the dark waymark. The lantern shrine: the shrine with its carved arch and worn step, six lit refuge tiles with the chest inside, four standing stones and a threshold out through the trees. A marked hero who reaches the shrine's light stops the Wolf's Lantern Hunt at its edge. Saves from earlier versions keep their generated maps.
- Custom tales end with a real boss: one from the landscape’s pool (Thornhound or Grey Wolf, Ridge Giant or Old Drake, Glass Eye or Tide Warden), named after the tale’s world and using a marked signature attack. Their chapters also draw on a wider set of painted enemies, so each chapter’s guardians differ. The built-in tales and earlier saves are unchanged.
- The Hollow Keepers are real. Each built-in tale ends with its own named Keeper: the Wolf at the Last Light, the Crown’s Dragon and the Tideglass Warden. Keepers are tougher, hit harder and never stall by guarding. Each announces a signature attack (Lantern Hunt, Lanternfire Breath, Breaking Tide) that is marked on the ground and lands on its next turn; step off the marked tiles to avoid it. A missed pounce leaves the wolf exposed, and the Warden yields when beaten. See RULES.md. Games saved before this keep the untitled Keeper.
- The play screen is rebuilt around the board: the scene fills the window and the controls float over it (chapter and objective, turn order, Party and Foes, the Game Master's latest line, the selected foe's card, the hero and action bar). The Journal drawer holds the log, the dialogue with the Game Master and the story's pace. A Scene / Map switch sits beside the zoom buttons.
- Dice no longer cover the board: your attack rolls from the foe's card in a small card above the action bar, the table's rolls queue in the same card with Pause and Next, and the board shows the hit or miss.
- Each chapter opens with the Game Master standing in the tale's place, after the route map in the built-in tales. Ask the Game Master is a full-screen scene with questions that fit the moment, and the game's own guidance now answers questions about the guardians, the chest and the gateway.
- Victory relights the whole road to the Waylantern beside the recap; defeat and custom tales show the Game Master instead. The hero sheet shows the hero's full figure, epithet and stats.
- Every dialog, the header, replay, notices and loading screens use the realm style.
- The three built-in tales share one setting, the Lantern Marches. The tale list and chapter card name it beside the region (Lanternwood, the Hollow Reach, Saltreach Harbour). Each chapter opens on a clue the party can see: the empty salt stall, the erased name under the arch carving, the stripped lamp cage. Victory relights the tale's road toward the Waylantern at Threeway Crossing. Quick-start stories follow Aether's light from waymark to waymark. Saves from earlier versions keep their original chapter text.
- In the built-in tales each chapter now opens on the map of the Lantern Marches: the stretch of road you just cleared relights, its waymark fills, and the Game Master's opening sits beside the map. Select the map to skip the animation; with reduced motion it shows the result directly. Custom tales keep the chapter card.
- The first chapter of each built-in tale is its landmark scene on the board, in both the scene view and the tactical map: Starfall Market with its empty salt stall, oil cart, last lamp and broken gateway; the broken arch with its carved wall and scraped name; the lantern pier with its stripped lamp cage, working lens, crates and quay stairs. The props block tiles like any wall, and the exit is the gateway, the arch or the stairs.
- The main menu shows a ruined castle above a misty valley, framed by a broken arch, with the title in Cinzel; it replaces the daytime woodland panorama.
- Headings throughout the game, and the title screen's logo and menu, use the EB Garamond typeface in place of Georgia. Interface text stays in Inter.
- Camp is a full-screen title menu: the scene fills the screen, the logo sits top left and the menu is a plain text list at the lower left with a highlight bar. Camp no longer shows the app header.
- Asking the narrator is now a short conversation: earlier questions and replies stay in view and survive closing the dialog, for the current adventure.
- Join party asks for the party code first and shows which party you are joining while you pick a hero; a refused code returns to the code step with your hero kept.
- Party camp shows the created story's title, the number of open seats, and a Share invite button where the device has a share sheet.
- Victory and defeat show a recap (shards, chapters, rounds, damage, critical hits, who fell) and a New quest button.
- Removed the unused vendored component library and the 19 packages only it needed, 284 style rules for classes nothing uses, and 13 redundant `!important` overrides.
- Removed the Pixel art style and the Paint/Pixel switch; the game now has a single hand-drawn style. A saved Pixel preference is ignored. Camp's header shows only the logo, because the title menu already offers the guide and settings.
- Story-first adventure creation: describe a premise and narrator, select or converse about traits, preview three narrator profiles in Paint and Pixel, shape an editable storyline, choose or customize a story-linked hero, then set the rules and begin. Custom traits receive category-fit feedback and replacement suggestions.
- Fresh narrator auditions use generated sample text and persona-specific delivery. Live auditions require the existing team unlock, paid-audio opt-in and shared allowance. Selected narrator identity, voice direction, reviewed story, key locations and hero recipe persist through gameplay, saves and replay.

- Team-password AI access in Table settings → AI. Remembered devices default to enabled, retain their on/off choice, and can forget access. Server checks cover every paid route; the API key never reaches visitors.
- Shared hosted budget controls support $25/month, $2/day and $0.50/campaign, while local defaults stay conservative. Added private developer key/password setup and additive access migrations.

- Optional server-only Luna campaign recipes, narration and legal suggestions, plus bounded cached Realtime speech. Paid generation remains off by default; persisted captions and engine-authoritative gameplay survive presentation changes and reloads.
- Clearer local narration fallback messaging and an authorization recheck before returning in-flight suggestions or audio to a revoked seat.
- Release validation covers fresh local HTTP-route speech/playback and two-seat multiplayer; hosted generation/playback checks also passed; audible fidelity remains unverified. See AI-VALIDATION.md.

- Compact settings with stable section navigation, grouped sound controls and 44px close target. Appearance includes art style, world view, color, accent and motion.
- Optional Hex Settlers hover/click sounds with independent interface volume, preview, hover/focus toggle and recoverable playback errors. Audio levels migrate without enabling sound automatically.
- Consistent interface motion and a complete in-app Reduced override. Attack dialogs focus Roll d20; queued results preserve one dialog/backdrop, and dismissal controls share the exit transition.
- One keyboard destination in the board's Tab order, directional previews and Enter/Space movement. Camera framing stays bounded after manual zoom and resize; terrain and depth calculations avoid unnecessary work during interaction.
- More readable action labels and supply counts, consistent HP/result colors, visible replay selection and persistent quest-action emphasis.

- Player-triggered attack dice and queued results for other players, companions, enemies, and death saves.
- Pause/resume shared-roll playback or return directly to the board; recent results remain in the adventure log. Incoming rolls wait while another table dialog or mobile drawer is open.
- Persistent mobile turn guidance, legal-target counts, and clear next steps when movement or actions are exhausted.
- Chest and gateway controls reflect proximity and show “Finish adventure” at the final gateway. Waiting players retain their own power labels and supply counts.

## 0.1.0-alpha.1 — 2026-11-02

First shared repository checkpoint of the existing Painted Realms prototype.

- Solo play, scripted companions and online multiplayer for up to ten heroes.
- Seeded three-chapter adventures with streamlined and Classic SRD-inspired rules.
- Paint and Pixel art with independent 2D/2.5D views.
- Responsive interface, light/dark modes, accent colors and configurable controls.
- Saved adventures, replay, browser narration and synthesized background music.
- Team contribution guide, roadmap and automated type/test/build checks.

Known scope: paid AI generation is not connected; the rules are a custom subset; saved-game compatibility may change during alpha. Hosting the source on GitHub does not automatically redeploy the game.
