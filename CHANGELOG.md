# Changelog

Record user-visible changes at version checkpoints. Git history and pull requests track the individual edits between them.

## Unreleased

- Make your own hero: the Hero step's last card opens a builder for a name, one of the eight classes, a colour and who they are. With painting on, paint them too: the picture stands on the board, in the turn order, on their sheet and in replays. Each player gets 2 Game Master pictures and 2 hero pictures a day.

- Paint your own Game Master: with AI on, the builder can turn your choices and a few words into a picture in the realm's style (FLUX with the team's style LoRA). Each try paints two; keep one or keep the drawing. Up to 4 pictures per player per day. Descriptions can't name characters from other stories (describe the look instead), a crown replaces the hat rather than sitting on it, and a picture with the figure cut off at the edge isn't offered. The picture stands in every scene, card and replay; the drawing stays as the fallback.

- Your own Game Master, and Game Masters made with the AI, are now drawn as full figures in the same style as the Wanderer, the Survivor and the Cursed One, instead of the soft painted portrait with orbiting sparks. Pick a person, spirit, cat, owl, fox or dragon; their colour dresses them, glasses and crowns are drawn on, and how they tell it sets their silhouette, hat and what they carry: a wide hat and a lantern for hope, a feathered cap, a book and a tossed d20 for a smile, a tall hat, high collar and candle for a cost. Every one keeps a pouch of dice. They stand in their scene at setup, the chapter opening, Ask, the tale conversation and the ending, and their face sits in the play rail and journal like the built-ins.
- Companions and heroes outside the roster have their own drawings instead of borrowing a roster hero's painting. Each is drawn in the roster's style from their name, class and colour: a face, skin, hair and build of their own, and their class's gear in one of several poses (a raised sword, a tower shield, a falcon on the arm, a raven on the shoulder, a prayer book, a lute or a song). The same companion looks the same on the board in both views, in the turn order, on their sheet, at camp, in the journal and in replays. The party's Pip, a ranger, is no longer drawn as the roster's rogue.
- The tactical map is painted from above in the scene's palette: ground, paths and water join their neighbours, walls are one stone mass per region with a cast shadow, trees are canopies, and every landmark, the chest and the gateway are drawn from overhead. Figures are round tokens cut from their own paintings, so the eel, the crab and every hero look the same in both views. The move range there is a softer dotted outline.
- Your own Game Master no longer offers the drawn hood; a saved hood simply shows nothing.
- The game has sound. Each region has its own music by day and after dusk, the Keepers bring their own theme, a fallen hero brings peril, and the camp, the road map, victory and defeat each have theirs. Beneath it, birds and wind in Lanternwood, wind on the Hollow Reach and waves and gulls at Saltreach; crickets after dusk; drips in the vaults. On the table: dice that land as the roll card settles, footsteps on grass, stone, planks and water, a sound for every class's blow and power, a voice for each creature family, Keeper warnings and strikes, the chest, the shard's bell and the gateway, and the road chiming as it relights. Books, pages, notes, the seal and the menus sound like the objects they are. Settings › Sound has Music, Sounds of play, Surroundings and Table sounds, each with its level. All files are CC0 and credited (public/audio/CREDITS.md); SOUND.md is the plan.
- The chapter opening and Ask the Game Master now stand in the tale's region instead of the tale painting: the road to Starfall Market in Lanternwood, the broken arch of the Hollow Reach, the lantern pier at Saltreach Harbour. By day you see the place in daylight; at night (Time of day) the same view after dusk, lit by its lamps, windows and the working lens.
- The creator's guide keeps the name Aether: it is the voice of the light the party follows from waymark to waymark.
- The road during play: in the built-in tales, *The road* beside Journal opens the map of the Lantern Marches mid-chapter, showing how far the road is lit, where the party stands and the shards recovered, then *Back to the table*. The lantern shards beside the objective open it too, and on phones they are the way in. Nothing relights and the game doesn't pause.
- The replay is a theatre now: no website header, the tale's name and chapter over the board, the narration line under it in the storyteller's italic, and a playback bar whose timeline is the road with a waymark for each chapter (select one to jump there). Speed is a ‹ 1× › chooser instead of a drop-down, and the side shows the party with health bars and the tale so far. The Storyteller page in Table settings uses the menu's rows: Team AI on or off, the shared limits as three figures, and the steps in plain words. Rename and Delete on Adventures are notes on the sealed note's parchment. Ask Pip and the Game Master link only appear in settings during a game, where they lead somewhere. In the creator and the menus, buttons that already show their words no longer repeat them in a tooltip; icon-only buttons keep theirs.
- Adventures, Table settings and the Adventurer's guide are game screens now, not web pages. **Adventures** is a save-slot screen: the chosen tale's painting fills the screen, each save is a slot with its waymarks along the road and *Your turn* glowing like a lamp, pinned saves carry a red ribbon, and the chosen save shows its place, the latest line of the story, your hero and the road so far over the painting. *Begin a new quest* is the empty slot at the end. Camp, the guide and settings are words in the corners; ↑/↓ choose, Enter continues and Esc goes back to Camp. The search box appears once there are eight saves. **Table settings** is an options menu: Camp-style pages (The look, Sound, Controls, At the table, Storyteller), every option a ‹ value › chooser that ←/→ change, volumes and voice speed as ten notches, Colour mode renamed *Time of day* (Day, Night, Your device) and Accent colour *Banner colour* as glass gems. **The guide** is a field guide: an open book with cloth ribbons for Playing, Keys, Rules and Credits, your turn as three inked steps and each chapter's goal as four stations; phones show one page at a time. Every action and setting is kept.
- The hero sheet's *Break the seal* link shows again as red ink on the note's parchment; the table's general button style had turned it into a dark, clipped button.
- Long built-in tales now play five distinct places instead of repeating the first two: chapter I, chapter II, two new places on the road, then the Keeper's place. The Lantern Road adds Bramble Crossing (a stream with a new plank bridge, stepping stones and brambles cut back from one bank) and the Moonwell (a shining well in a ring of standing stones, its lantern hook empty); the Hollow Crown adds the Sunken Archive (flooded channels between shelves, one emptied) and the Star Chamber (a brass orrery missing a star); Salt and Silver adds the Tideglass Steps (terraces down to the harbour, fresh glass on every step) and the Mistwater Cove (a smugglers' boat beached with its lantern hooded). Each opens on its own clue. Three new creatures guard them: the Mirewisp, the Star moth and the Mist eel. The between-chapters map shows the party on the road to the third waymark. Saves started earlier keep their places.
- Create your own Game Master: a fourth card on the Game Master step. Name them (or borrow a name), say who they are, choose how they tell it (Hopeful, Wry or Grave) and their shape, what they wear and their colour, with a live preview and a sample line. They tell any tale in the Lantern Marches with the matching narration voice and appear in the setup, the chapter openings and play. The result passes the same validation as an AI-made Game Master; the built-in names stay reserved, and heroes keep no sealed notes with your own Game Master.
- On phones the selected foe's card is two compact rows in the Game Master's slot instead of a large panel over the board: face, name, health and the attack buttons, then the facts as chips in the order that decides an attack (a Keeper's next move, range, to hit, damage, armour). The fight and the board tools stay in view.
- Sealed notes. When a built-in hero sets out with a built-in Game Master, the Game Master hands that hero's player a sealed letter once chapter I's opening closes: break the wax seal to read the secret that ties the hero to the Game Master (one of 48 from the Story Bible). Only that player sees it; companions and custom heroes or Game Masters have none. Later keeps it sealed, and the note stays in your hero sheet either way.
- In dark colour mode the board is at night. The ground is moonlit and a cool veil darkens it toward the edges; every light already on the board (the last lamp, the lit waymark, the shrine, the keep's torches and crown, the district lamp, the lens, the sea door) keeps a halo and throws a warm or teal pool. A hero or foe standing in a pool takes its light as a rim; props out of the light sit in moonlight. Move range, the Keepers' marked tiles, the refuge edge, the chest, the way on and health bars stay above the night. Light mode is unchanged, and night changes no rule.
- The Hollow Crown's and Salt and Silver's chapters II and III are now their approved places. The ridge road: the trail along the drop behind a patched parapet with two gaps, a retaining wall below, banners in the wind and cart ruts to the turn. The dragon now draws its head back with its throat lit when it marks the lane, and shows its open jaws after breathing; the Warden winds up with her blade across her body and kneels when she yields. Saves from earlier versions keep their generated maps.

- The Lantern Road's chapters II and III are now their approved places. The forest road: raised cobbles with a stone lip, a split-rail fence with one gap, the lit and the dark waymark, low water on both sides that blocks movement but not sight, and the road running out between two trunks; the wolf's eyes watch from beyond the dark waymark. - Custom tales end with a real boss: one from the landscape’s pool (Thornhound or Grey Wolf, Ridge Giant or Old Drake, Glass Eye or Tide Warden), named after the tale’s world and using a marked signature attack. Their chapters also draw on a wider set of painted enemies, so each chapter’s guardians differ. The built-in tales and earlier saves are unchanged.
- The Hollow Keepers are real. Each built-in tale ends with its own named Keeper: the Wolf at the Last Light, the Crown’s Dragon and the Tideglass Warden. Keepers are tougher, hit harder and never stall by guarding. Each announces a signature attack (Lantern Hunt, Lanternfire Breath, Breaking Tide) that is marked on the ground and lands on its next turn; step off the marked tiles to avoid it. A missed pounce leaves the wolf exposed, and the Warden yields when beaten. See RULES.md. Games saved before this keep the untitled Keeper.

## 0.1.0-alpha.1 — 2026-11-02

First shared repository checkpoint of the existing Painted Realms prototype.

- Solo play, scripted companions and online multiplayer for up to ten heroes.
- Seeded three-chapter adventures with streamlined and Classic SRD-inspired rules.
- Paint and Pixel art with independent 2D/2.5D views.
- Responsive interface, light/dark modes, accent colors and configurable controls.
- Saved adventures, replay, browser narration and synthesized background music.
- Team contribution guide, roadmap and automated type/test/build checks.

Known scope: paid AI generation is not connected; the rules are a custom subset; saved-game compatibility may change during alpha. Hosting the source on GitHub does not automatically redeploy the game.
